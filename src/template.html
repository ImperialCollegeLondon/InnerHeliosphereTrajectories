About

Frame. Heliocentric inertial (HCI): the solar equatorial plane, with longitude measured from the ascending node of the solar equator on the J2000 ecliptic (i = 7.25°, Ω = 75.76°). Latitude is heliographic.

Fixed longitude. Choosing an object subtracts its longitude from every object's, so it sits at 0° and the others drift relative to it. Latitudes are never altered. Co-rotating instead fixes the frame to the Sun's 25.38-day sidereal rotation.

Data. Sampled daily, refined to 4-hourly and hourly where the objects move fastest, then interpolated with a cubic spline — good to ~0.01° of longitude through Parker's closest perihelion. Times are UTC (Horizons TDB, offset ~69 s).

† Predicted. Beyond the end of an object's SPK its marker is drawn as a dashed ring and its readout carries a †; the coverage bars hatch the same stretch.

L1 is Earth's position moved 1.5 million km sunward; its longitude and latitude are Earth's.

Fine time control. The range box by the slider sets how much time the slider spans — from the full 13 years down to 24 hours, so the slider can be as precise as you need. The strip beneath it always shows the whole mission: tap or drag it to jump anywhere, and the highlighted box marks the slider's current window. The ◀ ▶ buttons step by an amount that follows the range (1 day when zoomed out, down to 5 minutes at 24 hours).
